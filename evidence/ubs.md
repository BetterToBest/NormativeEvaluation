> This criterion asks whether the system structurally prevents wealth *concentration* — a Gini-coefficient-relevant question, distinct from C1.2a's resilience-buffer question. This is the single most consequential call in this evaluation, and it was revised once during drafting, disclosed here rather than silently settled. **The case for 0.5** (this evaluation's initial read): the shelter sector's plan to double the UK's social housing stock by 1.5 million units removes that many households from the private rental market entirely, a real, costed, committed mechanism — unlike Doughnut Economics, which was scored 0.0 here specifically for citing compatible redistributive mechanisms without adopting any of its own, UBS *does* adopt a specific mechanism of its own, which seemed to warrant credit above Doughnut Economics' clean zero, on the model of Georgism's own 0.5 for land value capture. **Why this evaluation lands at 0.0 instead:** on closer comparison with Georgism's actual mechanism, the analogy does not hold. Georgism's land value tax *captures wealth that would otherwise accumulate to landowners* — it is a device that removes an existing, ongoing accumulation from one party's hands. The proposal's funding mechanism (reducing the Personal Allowance) is a tax-and-transfer adjustment, not a wealth tax, asset cap, or land-value-capture device either. On reflection, this evaluation concludes UBS has no C1.2b mechanism of the kind this criterion asks about, at any scale — a different, and weaker, case than either Georgism's or Mutual Credit/LETS's own targeted mechanisms. *(The section continues at the link.)*
