# NEEC Step 1c: Material Security Retrofit (C1.2 → C1.2a/C1.2b, C1.5 narrowed)

**Step 1c retrofit deliverable — Session 8; its retrofitted scores have been
canonical since Session 8.** Retrofits all 13 systems previously scored
under NEEC's legacy 25-criterion structure (Report Systems 1–12; Integral,
Paper Appendix E) to the v2, 26-criterion structure specified in full in
Paper Section 12.3 and anchored in Appendix H.7v2. This is the "dedicated
future pass" Section 12.5 explicitly deferred: *"a full re-review of each
system's existing evidence against the new criterion boundaries... checking,
system by system, whether the evidence that supported the old C1.2 score
speaks to C1.2a, C1.2b, or both, and separately re-checking whether
narrowing C1.5 changes any of its 13 published scores."*

**Method, stated plainly.** Per Section 12.5, this is a *re-review of
already-published evidence*, not new research. Every score below is derived
from that system's own already-published C1.2 and C1.5 rationale text
(Report Part I; Paper Appendix E for Integral), cross-checked in every case
against C4.4 (Power Distribution) wherever C4.4 discusses wealth Gini or
concentration specifically — a source of relevant evidence Appendix H.7v2's
own C1.2b anchors already draw on but which the legacy C1.2/C1.5 write-ups
did not always cite directly. No system's C1.1, C1.3, C1.4, or any Domain
2–5 criterion is touched; this is confirmed programmatically below (see
Verification, Check 2).

**Confidence tags**, matching this project's established disclosure norms
(cf. Georgism's C4.3, Mutual Credit/LETS's C4.3 and C5.2):
- **anchored** — directly matches an existing Appendix H.7v2 worked anchor
  (several of H.7v2's own examples were written using these exact 13
  systems, so several scores below simply apply an anchor already stated
  in the Paper verbatim to the system it was illustrating)
- **direct** — follows straightforwardly from already-published rationale
  text, no anchor needed
- **inferential** — a defensible reading that goes somewhat beyond directly
  quoted figures (most often: no specific $ or % figure exists in the
  legacy text, so the score rests on general knowledge of the named
  mechanism's real-world character)
- **contestable** — a genuinely close call, flagged explicitly for
  independent disagreement

Two calls are flagged **contestable and consequential** and get their own
subsections in Part 2 below, because they are the only two calls in this
entire retrofit where a different reasonable judgment would change a
system's *adequacy tier* or *dominance-relevant profile*.

### 1. Status Quo Market Capitalism

**Legacy text.** C1.2 (0.5): *"The bottom 50% hold only ~$3,200 median
wealth despite decades of economic growth. While wealth accumulation
mechanisms exist (homeownership, retirement accounts), access concentrates
among those already wealthy, failing universal access requirement."* C1.5
(0.5): *"Only 60% have any wealth accumulation pathway; Gini coefficient
0.85 indicates extreme inequality."*

**C1.2a — 0.5 (Partial)** · *direct*. Homeownership and retirement accounts
are real, functioning accumulation vehicles — the legacy text doesn't
dispute the mechanism itself works, only that it "concentrates among those
already wealthy." But the same text's own bottom-50%-median figure
($3,200) is dramatically below the $60k+/20yr threshold, and nothing in
the corpus suggests 70%+ of participants clear it. A real, standard-market
mechanism reaching a meaningful but sub-threshold share.

**C1.2b — 0.0 (Structural Failure)** · *anchored*. Gini 0.85 is Appendix
H.7v2's own explicit 0.0 anchor case for this exact criterion — no
structural prevention mechanism exists beyond ordinary (and, per this
system's own C4.4, largely ineffective) progressive taxation.

**C1.5′ — 0.5 (Partial)** · *direct*. 60% access-breadth figure carried
forward unchanged; below the ≥80% threshold but a real, non-trivial share.

**Domain 1: 2.0/5 → 2.0/6.** No change in the raw sum (0.5 split into
0.5+0.0 preserves the total; C1.5 unchanged). **Total: 10.5/25 (42%) →
10.5/26 (40%).** **Failures: 8 → 9** (C1.2b joins C1.4 as Domain 1's
second zero). **Tier: unchanged** (Structurally Inadequate, still ≥6).

### 2. Nordic Social Democracy

**Legacy text.** C1.2 (0.5): *"Strong middle class with broader wealth
distribution (Gini ~0.65-0.75) than pure capitalism, but still falls short
of universal wealth-building access."* C1.5 (1.0): *"Approximately 70% have
wealth accumulation pathways through homeownership programs, pension
systems, and savings incentives. While not universal, it meets the
threshold for broad-based access."*

**C1.2a — 1.0 (Pass)** · *inferential*. The named mechanisms — multi-decade
pension systems, homeownership programs, savings incentives — are robust
institutional vehicles; the legacy 0.5's actual stated concern ("falls
short of universal... access") is about *breadth/distribution*, which is
now C1.5′/C1.2b's job, not about whether the mechanism is adequate for
those who engage it. This is flagged as more inferential than the
Market Socialism/CCO-PTF 1.0 cases below: no specific $ figure appears in
the existing text, so this reads real institutional strength into a 1.0
rather than citing a number. A more conservative reasoner might keep this
at 0.5; noted for independent re-check.

**C1.2b — 0.5 (Partial)** · *direct*. Wealth Gini 0.65–0.75 (the legacy
C1.2 figure, and the exact figure Appendix H.7v2 already discusses at
length for this system) is a meaningful reduction from unregulated
capitalism's 0.85 via genuine redistributive policy, but well above the
<0.35 threshold — matches H.7v2's own 0.5-band description almost exactly
("real, meaningful reduction... without a structural mechanism confirmed to
hold it below the threshold").

**C1.5′ — 1.0 (Pass)** · *anchored*. Appendix H.7v2 already works this
exact case: *"the same qualitative-tolerance judgment call already
documented in H.7's own C1.5 entry above carries over unchanged, since it
was never about the Gini clause."*

**Domain 1: 4.0/5 → 5.0/6.** **Total: 18.5/25 (74%) → 19.5/26 (75%).**
**Failures: 2 → 2** (no new zeros in Domain 1). **Tier: unchanged**
(Potentially Adequate).

## Part 2: The two contestable, consequential calls

Every other score above is disclosed with a confidence tag but landed on a
single considered judgment. These two get fuller treatment because each is
the deciding factor for something structurally significant — one for a
system's adequacy *tier*, the other for the internal consistency of a
finding this retrofit would otherwise report as settled.

### 2.1 MMT + Job Guarantee's C1.2b — the only tier-crossing call in this retrofit

**The stakes.** If C1.2b = 0.0, MMT+JG has 3 Domain 1 zeros where it
previously had 0, moving it from 2 failures (Potentially Adequate) to 3
(Partially Adequate) — the *only* tier change this entire 13-system
retrofit produces. If C1.2b = 0.5 instead, MMT+JG stays at 2 failures and
remains Potentially Adequate.

**The case for 0.0 (this evaluation's call).** Nothing in MMT+JG's design
targets wealth *concentration* specifically — no wealth tax, no asset cap,
no land-value capture, no abolition of private capital. The job guarantee
is an *income* and *employment* mechanism; it doesn't touch existing
accumulated wealth stocks or the rate at which capital owners' holdings
grow. This matches the same "no structural mechanism = 0.0" pattern this
retrofit applies to Status Quo Capitalism, UBI, and Stakeholder Capitalism
— MMT+JG's C1.2a is scored identically to Status Quo's for the same
underlying reason (same traditional-market mechanism), and the parallel
extends naturally to C1.2b.

**The case for 0.5 (the counter-consideration).** This system's own C4.4
scores 0.5, not 0.0, citing: *"Reduces capital power by providing an exit
option from private exploitation."* A reviewer could read "reduces capital
power" as sufficient evidence of *some* structural effect on wealth
concentration, clearing the bar for C1.2b's 0.5 band ("real, meaningful
reduction... without a structural mechanism confirmed to hold it below the
threshold").

**Why this evaluation doesn't adopt that reading.** C4.4's "reduces capital
power" is explicitly about *bargaining and political power* — the exit
option strengthens workers' negotiating position and the guarantee itself
expands the state's economic role — not about a policy instrument that
targets the *Gini coefficient of wealth* the way Degrowth's caps, the
various private-capital-abolition systems, or Georgism's land tax do. An
improved bargaining position could, over time and indirectly, produce
somewhat better wage outcomes (already captured in C1.1/C2.1), but that is
a different causal channel than C1.2b's own threshold, which is about
structural prevention of *asset* concentration specifically.

**Disclosure.** This is presented as the single most consequential close
call in this retrofit, not a settled fact. A future scorer who finds the
C4.4-documented capital-power reduction sufficient for C1.2b's threshold
should feel free to override this call to 0.5 — doing so removes MMT+JG's
only tier change and nothing else in this retrofit depends on this
specific number.

### 2.2 Centrally Planned Socialism's C1.2b — the nomenklatura tension

**The case for 1.0.** Abolition of private capital markets should produce
an extremely low *measured* monetary wealth Gini — there was no large-
scale financial-asset ownership to concentrate. This was this retrofit's
initial read.

**Why it was revised to 0.5.** This system's own already-published C4.4
rationale is explicit: *"Extreme power concentration in party apparatus and
planning bureaucracy. Attempted to eliminate the capitalist class but
created a new elite (nomenklatura) with concentrated economic and
political power."* "Concentrated *economic* power" — not just political
power — is the system's own already-published finding, describing real,
well-documented privilege (housing, special stores, foreign travel) that a
narrow, formal wealth-Gini reading would miss entirely, since none of it
passed through priced markets. A clean 1.0 would let the *absence of legal
private property* stand in for genuine concentration-prevention, when the
system's own C4.4 already establishes that concentration re-emerged
through a different channel.

**Why 0.5, not 0.0.** The *formal* wealth Gini genuinely was very
compressed — there is no comparably-documented case of individual
financial fortunes accumulating the way Status Quo Capitalism's or even
Nordic Social Democracy's did. 0.5 tries to hold both facts at once:
extremely compressed *formal* distribution, but real, already-documented
*de facto* concentration through a non-monetary channel.

**Disclosure.** Unlike the MMT+JG call above, this one does not change
any tier (Centrally Planned Socialism is at 12 failures either way, deep
in Structurally Inadequate territory) — it is flagged for the sake of
getting the number right and modeling the disclosure norm, not because the
stakes are as high.

## Part 3: What this retrofit confirms about the framework itself

**Integral's own case, now quantified.** Section 12.1 motivated this split
by observing that Integral's ITC design "is arguably close to the
strongest possible answer to (b), and the same design choice is what
produces its failure on (a)." The retrofit turns this from a qualitative
claim into an arithmetic one: Integral's Domain 1 goes from a weak
2.0/5 (40%) under the legacy conflated criterion to a much stronger
3.0/6 (50%) once C1.2b's genuine strength (1.0) is no longer averaged
together with C1.2a's genuine weakness (0.0). The split does exactly the
analytical work it was designed to do.

**Nordic Social Democracy and Integral land at an exact tie — twice.**
Under the legacy structure, these two systems were already tied at
18.5/25 (74%), a tie Appendix A.4 discusses at length as illustrating the
adequacy/feasibility tension (Section 12.4). Under the retrofit, they are
tied *again* — this time at 19.5/26 (75%) — even though each system's
underlying Domain 1 arithmetic moved independently (Nordic: 4.0→5.0;
Integral: 2.0→3.0) to produce the same relative outcome. Their tiers still
differ (Nordic: 2 failures, Potentially Adequate; Integral: 3 failures,
Partially Adequate, unchanged by the retrofit) — the tie persists at the
percentage level while the tier-defining failure counts stay exactly where
they were, which is a clean illustration that percentage and failure-count
are measuring different things by design (Paper Section 10.3), now with a
second independent data point.

**A genuine three-way tier/percentage crossover, for the first time.**
Prior to this session, the tier-vs-percentage tension had one clean
illustration (Georgism: 52%, Potentially Adequate — the *lowest* percentage
in that tier) and one separate illustration one session later (Mutual
Credit/LETS: 56%, Partially Adequate — a *higher* percentage than Georgism,
in a *worse* tier). This retrofit adds a third data point in the same
comparative space: **MMT + Job Guarantee, 60%, Partially Adequate** — a
higher percentage than both Georgism and Mutual Credit/LETS, still in the
worse tier relative to Georgism. Laid out together:

| System | Percentage | Failures | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Georgism / Land Value Tax | 52% | 2 | **Potentially Adequate** |
| Mutual Credit / LETS | 56% | 3 | Partially Adequate |
| MMT + Job Guarantee | 60% | 3 | Partially Adequate |
| Integral | 75% | 3 | Partially Adequate |

Georgism has the *lowest* percentage of the four but the *best* tier — the
cleanest concentrated illustration yet of Section 8.3's failure-count
classification diverging from Section 10.3's own point about why NEEC treats
scalar rank as its least important comparative tool. **Precisely stated: in
Session 8 the Partially Adequate tier had three members** (Mutual
Credit/LETS, MMT+JG, Integral, spanning 56–75%), **with Georgism sitting
just below them in percentage while remaining in the better, Potentially
Adequate tier** — it is this adjacency, not co-membership, that makes the
four-system table above an instructive unit. On the canonical corpus the
tier has eight members — MMT + Job Guarantee, Integral, Mutual Credit /
LETS, Universal Basic Services, Sovereign Wealth Fund Statism, State
Capitalism / Singapore, Islamic finance and Ostrom-style commons governance
— spanning 52% to 75%: a meaningfully richer basis than the single-member
tier that existed before Session 8 (Integral alone) — flagged again for the
"Partially Adequate tier formalization" task already queued in the project
roadmap, now with enough real data to actually do that work.

**A second, independent percentage/tier tie worth flagging.** Universal
Basic Income and Mutual Credit/LETS land at an *exact* percentage tie (both
14.5/26, 56%) on opposite sides of the Structurally-Inadequate/
Partially-Adequate boundary (7 failures vs. 3 respectively) — arguably a
sharper illustration than the four-system table above, since it is an exact
tie rather than mere proximity. See Verification, below, for where the
ranking is printed.

**Dominance relations: a validation, not a new finding.** Running the
pairwise dominance check under the retrofitted structure (in Session 8, on
the 15-system corpus) shows CCO-PTF-CIP-SZH failing to *strictly* dominate
several systems the Paper's own Section 11.3 already discusses only under
