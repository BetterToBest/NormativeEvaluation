## 2. How a clause is recorded, and the rule

As in part (a), section 2: each clause is cleared, short, not shown, out of reach or moot; a 1.0 stands only if every
clause is cleared or moot, and otherwise becomes 0.5 (D28, protocol 2.3). The clauses are v2.0's, in their Pass
Threshold's order (`criteria.json`; protocol draft.8, Appendix B). Each is mapped to the Session 44 clause it
continues and inherits that clause's audit code: C4.2's four in order, C4.4's two from the Session 44 clauses 3 and 4.
A clause the audit coded A, on a clause v2.0 keeps verbatim, is carried as cleared on the unit's own text ("as
audited") unless the entry's or design's own sources contradict it; no clause here needs that exception. On a clause
v2.0 restates, an A code is read again against the restated clause (reading 3.2): six are carried ("as audited,
re-read") and one is estimated. One clause is marked "not estimated in this pass": Degrowth's C4.2 regeneration
clause, in a unit its biodiversity clause fixes; it stays not shown, and Report v2.0's clause-level Part I must
estimate it. No clause is out of reach: the audit's only reach codes here, MMT's four on C4.2, were superseded in
part (a) under D31, and the restatement changes neither.

## 3. Decisions and readings (under the delegation)

**3.1 The group, and the structure its totals are on.** The units are listed in the audit register's order, then

**3.2 Audit codes on restated clauses.** (1) *Interpretations.* The audit read its phrases against the Session 44
wording. On a clause v2.0 restates, an A code could (a) lapse, the clause then being estimated as a silent clause is,
or (b) be read again: the audited phrase is tested against the restated clause under the audit's own standard for A
(the text claims the condition holds, in terms compatible with the bar; a qualitative statement counts, and so do
hedged projections for designs), carried where it claims the restated condition and estimated otherwise.
(2) *Preponderance:* (b). Decision 7.1 moved the pass onto the revised clauses so that each unit is scored once, not
so that restated clauses are held to a different standard from verbatim ones. Neither restatement lowers the bar or
changes the clause's subject: the carbon rate is the rate of the old clause's 35% cut over 2019–2030, and the four
downscaled boundaries are among the nine. Reading (a) would re-audit only the clauses v2.0 happened to reword.
(3) *Score* against (b). A phrase claiming that economic activity stays within planetary boundaries claims that no
more than two of the four are transgressed: Degrowth ("explicitly bounded by planetary limits"), Fully Automated
Luxury Communism ("harmonize human activity with planetary boundaries"), Participatory Economics ("within ecological
limits"), Integral ("enforce planetary-boundary compliance") and Doughnut Economics (the planetary-boundaries ceiling
"incorporated as a hard structural design element") are carried on clause 4. A phrase claiming absolute reductions
reached by lowering consumption claims a fall in consumption-based emissions: Degrowth's "designed for absolute
reductions", which its rationale sets against relative efficiency and whose means is less throughput in rich
economies, is carried on clause 1. CCO-PTF-CIP-SZH's phrase, a "35-45% reduction trajectory achievable with carbon tax
at $50-100/ton", states a size without a horizon, so it claims no rate; the clause is estimated from the design's own
figures (reading 3.4). (4) *Alternative recorded:* reading (a) changes no verdict, since every C4.2 unit falls on
clause 2; it would leave six more clauses for Report v2.0 to estimate.

**3.3 C4.2, clause 2, for designs: biodiversity.** (1) *Interpretations.* "Biodiversity neutral or positive" could be
shown (a) by a projected outcome, or (b) by a design that names biodiversity among its limits or its metrics.
(2) *Preponderance:* (a). v2.0's measurement field says what a design must show, "the projected pressure on species
and habitat", and that "each clause is projected on the same measure". Part (b1)'s reading 3.5 holds that a design's
specification of an institution is not an estimate of the level, and part (b6)'s reading 3.3 that a boundary to
respect is not a reduction delivered. (3) *Score:* the clause is shown by a modelled or component-calibrated
projection of pressure on species and habitat under the design. (4) *Applied:* none of the six has one. Degrowth's own
scenario researchers write that "to date, degrowth scenarios have not been explored for biodiversity conservation and
human wellbeing", and set out the steps by which they could be (Otero et al., Sustainability Science, 2024); the
IPBES Global Assessment, as they cite it, finds degrowth's transformative potential for biodiversity high but the
evidence of its effectiveness still inconclusive. Fully Automated Luxury Communism argues that cultured food needs a
fraction of livestock's land, conditional on the technology; Participatory Economics prices ecological damage in
planning; CCO-PTF-CIP-SZH lists biodiversity among the metrics to monitor; Integral couples production to ecological
thresholds; Doughnut Economics' ceiling includes biodiversity breakdown, a goalpost whose global overshoot its authors
monitor. Each names biodiversity or means to protect it; none projects the pressure. *Reopening:* a modelled
projection of pressure on species and habitat under any of these designs, such as the degrowth biodiversity scenarios
Otero et al. call for.

**3.4 C4.2, clause 1, for designs: carbon as a rate.** Part (b6)'s reading 3.3 left open whether "35% carbon
reduction by 2030" was a deadline or a rate. v2.0 settles it: the clause is a rate, "Consumption-based carbon
emissions falling ≥3.8% a year", which binds at any date, on consumption-based accounts (G3). For a design it is shown
by a projected rate on that measure. *Applied:* Fully Automated Luxury Communism, Participatory Economics, Integral and
Doughnut Economics state no emissions path, as part (b6) found on C4.1's carbon clause, and the rate form does not
change that. CCO-PTF-CIP-SZH's model reports total emissions "45% below baseline trajectory over 20 years", of which
consumption-driven emissions fall 24%; with the baseline unstated no absolute rate follows (against a flat baseline,
45% over 20 years is about 2.9% a year, below the bar), and the roadmap's figures, 15-20% in year 1 rising to 70-80%
in year 7, are targets in a table that does not say whose emissions it covers (part (b6), reading 3.7). Not shown.

**3.5 C4.4, clause 2: "removal/replacement mechanisms functional".** (1) *Interpretations.* The clause could ask (a)
for a record of removals in operation, or (b) for a mechanism the system specifies by which those subject to an
authority it creates can remove or replace the authority's holders, of a kind that functions where it is in use.
(2) *Preponderance:* (b). The Measurement line names "Accountability structures (capacity to remove/replace
decision-makers)", a capacity, and "functional" asks that the capacity be operative rather than nominal. A design has
no record, so (a) would fail every design by a test it cannot pass, the mirror of the test that cannot fail which
part (b1)'s reading 3.1 rejects. (3) *Score* against (b): shown by recall, election at fixed terms, term limits,
rotation, or sortition with limited terms, specified in the system's own sources for the holders of the authority it
creates; where a design delegates no authority to office-holders, by revocation of decisions by those they bind; for
a comprehensive system scored on its components, by the law in force for them. A commitment to democratic governance,
accountability or decentralisation is a goal, not a mechanism, and a mechanism supplied by the polity the system
operates in is not credited to it (part (b5), reading 3.3; protocol 3.2; D29(c)). (4) *Applied:* Participatory
Economics cleared: its councils "elect recallable and rotated representatives" to their federations. Integral
cleared: it delegates no managerial authority; decisions are taken by participants in CDS and can be reaffirmed,
amended, revoked or reopened by its review module, and production is coordinated by small rotating teams that, in the
white paper's words, "did not command; they coordinated", a function COS formalises (section 5.5). CCO-PTF-CIP-SZH
cleared and **flagged**: its governance paper gives Public Trust Housing leadership term limits, mandatory rotation
and recall elections, and its merit juries are selected at random and rotated; but the other Public Trust
Foundations' governance is stated as "tripartite governance structures ensuring community representation", with no
removal mechanism, and the currency's parameters run through host institutions, so a reading that asks for a
mechanism for every body the design creates leaves the clause not shown (alternative 0.5). Market Socialism cleared
(reading 3.7). Degrowth not shown: the entry names democratic governance as a goal, and degrowth research reflects on
"forms of technology and democracy compatible with degrowth", holding that "the conditions of its realization
require additional study" (Kallis et al., 2018).

**3.6 C4.4, clause 1, for a configured national economy.** Nordic Social Democracy's accountability clause, left not
estimated in part (a), now decides its unit. (1) *Interpretations.* "Democratic accountability for ≥80% of major
decisions" could be read (a) as accountability to those the decisions affect, across economic and political domains,
or (b) as decisions taken within law made by institutions answerable to voters. (2) *Preponderance:* (a). The
Requirement asks to "diffuse rather than concentrate decision-making authority across economic, political, and social
domains", and the Measurement line reads "accountability of major decisions to those they affect"; reading (b) would
clear every democracy on its constitution alone. (3) *Score:* no published indicator measures the share, and none
was located; the nearest, V-Dem's "power distributed by socioeconomic position", rates how far wealth and income
translate into political power. The entry's rationale names mechanisms (strong unions, proportional representation, worker board
representation), not a share. Public decisions answer to parliaments; the major decisions of private firms answer to
their owners, beside a minority of employee-elected board members (in Sweden two, or three at 1,000 employees).
Whether 80% of major decisions are accountable turns on how the two are weighed, which no source measures: not shown.
The unit stays 0.5, **flagged** toward 1.0 on reading (b). *Reopening:* a source measuring the share of major
economic and political decisions accountable to those they affect.

**3.7 Part (a)'s units, re-read on v2.0.** Part (a) scored MMT's C4.2 and Nordic Social Democracy's and Market
Socialism's C4.4 on the Session 44 clauses. v2.0 restates C4.2 and deletes C4.4's first two clauses, so each unit is
re-read on the v2.0 clauses, and the record here supersedes part (a)'s. C4.4 is a class D criterion, so its units can

removal clause (the Riksdag's confidence vote of 21 June 2021) and stays 0.5 on accountability (reading 3.6). Market
Socialism's C4.4 keeps its accountability clause as audited ("economic power distributed through democratic
ownership") and clears the removal clause on the law in force for its principal component: under the Basque
cooperatives law that governs Mondragon's cooperatives, administrators serve two to five years, and the general
assembly may dismiss them, even when dismissal is not on the agenda, and elect their replacements in the same session
(Ley 11/2019, article 46). The unit returns to its published 1.0.
