What the simulation finds

The math and code of this simulation were engineered by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, from the concepts in the author's book Better To Best and his related vision for eradicating extreme poverty while enriching cultures and supporting human flourishing. The model has not yet been reviewed by an independent economist; the full code is open for anyone to check, and expert collaborators are welcome.

The headline results come first. Then comes a guided read of each, with a chart, and then every table behind them, with downloads. Pick a release, an environment and 20 or 40 years. The address carries your choice, so you can share any view.

v5.6 (2026-10-14): Today’s safety net in every run: SNAP (with its work rule for adults without children under 14), the earned income tax credit and the refundable Child Tax Credit are paid in the no-program run and beside Compassionism, so the program is measured against today’s US policy; SNAP’s income limit is no longer read as a tax on all earnings.

What changed for the figures in v5.4. People can now lose their job and find another, as US workers do (flows and spell lengths from the Current Population Survey), with unemployment insurance in both runs. After a permanent job loss, pay on return is lower, by 10% on average. That is a design parameter, because no single source fixes it for every permanent loss. In Adverse and Stress, the automation wave now works through job loss in high-risk occupations, instead of slower pay for everyone. Community housing (PTH) keeps the trust’s books for every home. Acre Equity grows at most at the price rise plus 2% (the author’s cap, d175), and members who move away take 40% of their Acre Equity in cash (d174). Community businesses (PTF) now cut prices sector by sector instead of a flat 12%: food and utilities at the figures in the Basic Living Economic Index (BLEI) paper, and no cut where the Hub gives none. Members who move away leave. Job loss changes the no-program run as well as the program, so v5.4’s figures cannot be compared with earlier releases’: compare within a release. v5.3’s main row, run on the v5.4 engine with its own no-program run, is shown beside the main result as the reading “v5.3’s main row”. In Reference over 20 years, too little wealth changes by +6.1 points against no program there, and by +11.7 in the main result.

GUIDED READ

Results by environment and horizon #

This is Compassionism as the Research Hub specifies it, with every mechanism in, set against no program. A Source that issues the Basic Units (BU) pays for it. In the cautious main reading, what the Source pays out is new money, except the part that new output backs. Each environment is a different world the same adults live in: Reference (the model’s main settings, with no recessions and no outside inflation), Adverse (recessions, 2% outside inflation and an automation wave) and Stress Test (the Adverse environment with weaker settings (40% take part, a smaller allowance)). In this release both runs also have today’s safety net: SNAP (food stamps, with its work rule for adults aged 18 to 64 with no child under 14), the earned income tax credit and the refundable Child Tax Credit, paid on last year’s income. “No program” therefore means today’s US policy, and each change is what Compassionism adds to it.

GUIDED READ

Jobs, homes and shops, and how risky life is #

What v5.4 adds, for the environment and horizon chosen. Work: each year an adult at work can lose or leave their job (8.5% a year in normal years, 14.2% in recessions; flows from the Bureau of Labor Statistics Current Population Survey). They are out of work for a spell of the measured length, then come back. After a permanent loss (41% of spells), pay is lower by 10% on average. That cut is a design parameter: the readings show no cut and a 20% cut. Unemployment insurance is paid as in the US, in both runs. Community housing: each PTH (Public Trust Housing) home has the trust’s books: value, debt, upkeep, tax, the member’s Acre Equity and the community’s equity. The identity “homes minus debt equals members’ equity plus community equity” is checked for every home every year. Community businesses: a member’s price cut is built from sectors. Food gets the BLEI paper’s figure (62% off, less while under 40% of adults are members) and utilities 44% off. Transport, health care and childcare get no cut, because the Hub gives no figure. Risk: how far savings and days of basic living fall from their peak, how fast income recovers after a shock, and how often people fall back below the cost of living.

Against the Hub’s own targets #

The Research Hub sets two targets for Year 7 of a program: a poverty rate under 2% (from about 12%) and a Gini coefficient of 0.25 to 0.30 (from 0.48). A Gini coefficient measures inequality, from perfect equality to one person owning everything. Both targets are in the Integrated Implementation Roadmap (Success Metrics by Year 7; Appendix I). The Basic Living Economic Index (BLEI) paper adds 0.25 as its design target for the spread of wealth.

The decisive unknown: how much of the payout new output backs #

The decisive unknown is how much of the Source’s payout new output backs. Call that share a. The main row assumes none of the net payout is backed by new output (a = 0). The H1 reading assumes all of it is (a = 1). This sweep tries a at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 on the same 500 paired runs of 500 adults over 20 years, with every other mechanism as in the main row. The two ends are the main row and H1, matching to the last digit.

GUIDED READ

How far the answer moves: the readings beside the main row #

Each reading changes one assumption beside the main row, over the same paired runs. None of them is in the main row. The first group is v5.4’s: v5.3’s main row (no job loss, and community housing (PTH) and community businesses (PTF) as before), no pay cut on return to work, a 20% cut, automation as a lasting pay drag, the earlier Acre Equity rule (3–5% a year), PTF cutting 25% in the sectors the Hub gives no figure for, and a disaster in Year 7 (a scenario, not a part of the model). The second group undoes, one at a time, each choice v5.3 added: households, pooling, the child allowance, the wider list of what BU buy, starting wealth from the survey, and spending that follows income. A reading that changes the population or something outside the design (job loss and its pay rule, the disaster, households, savings, aging, the US-data readings) applies to the no-program run too. Its change is then measured against no program under the same rule.

GUIDED READ

The no-program run against US data #

The no-program run is the yardstick for every result, so it is checked against published US figures: poverty (Census Bureau, Poverty in the United States: 2025), income inequality (Census Bureau, Income in the United States: 2025), wealth (Federal Reserve, 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances: families with a head aged 25–66 and wages, by type, in 2025 dollars) and how poverty spells end (Panel Study of Income Dynamics, Stevens 1994). Households start with the survey’s wealth for their type, so the first year matches it by construction; the check is how the yardstick holds up over the years. No setting was changed to bring a later figure closer. This check is v5.3’s no-program run (before job loss joined it); job loss changes the no-program run, so rerunning the check is listed as follow-up work. It has no safety net (v5.6 added it to both runs); rerunning it with the safety net is listed as follow-up work.
