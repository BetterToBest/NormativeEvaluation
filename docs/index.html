NEEC compares economic systems against one fixed set of normative criteria, with the evidence behind every mark.

NEEC stands for Normative Economic Evaluation Criteria. Normative means the criteria say what an economy ought to achieve: security from poverty, freedom to choose, resilience in a crisis, fairness across groups and generations, and a credible way to get there. Each of the 23 systems is scored 1, 0.5 or 0 on each of the 29 criteria. A 0 is a structural failure, and failures, not totals, decide a system's tier.

Findings

  • 23 systems on 29 criteria: 7 Potentially Adequate, 7 Partially Adequate, 9 Structurally Inadequate. Tiers depend only on the number of structural failures, so they do not move with the weights.
  • Among the configured national economies, existing or historical, the highest total is Nordic Social Democracy's: 15.0 of 29 (52%), with 1 structural failure, Potentially Adequate.
  • A higher total can sit in a lower tier: Ostrom Commons (14.5, 3 failures) outscores Georgism / LVT (14.0, 2 failures) and sits one tier lower.
  • No system scores 1.0 on 13 of the 29 criteria: C1.1, C1.2a, C1.4, C2.1, C2.3, C2.4, C2.6, C3.1, C3.5, C3.6, C4.2, C4.3, C4.6.
  • The most often failed criterion is C1.5 Universal Wealth Access and C2.2 Labor Non-Necessity: 11 of 23 systems score 0.0 on it.
  • 15 systems are not dominated: no other system scores at least as high on every criterion and higher on one. CCO-PTF-CIP-SZH, Participatory Economics, Integral, Nordic Social Democracy, Market Socialism, Ostrom Commons, Sovereign Wealth Fund Statism, Mutual Credit / LETS, Islamic Finance, Status Quo Market Capitalism, Universal Basic Income, Fully Automated Luxury Communism, State Capitalism / Qatar, Libertarian Minarchism, Centrally Planned Socialism.

The findings as charts: totals and failures, domain profiles, and trade-offs.

Totals use equal weights and are one reading of the evidence, not a measurement. Each system's page registers the close calls a reasonable scorer could set differently, and the Thresholds page shows how the ranking moves when you weigh the domains differently.

Who made this

NEEC is written by Duke Johnson, an independent researcher, and Claude (Anthropic). Duke Johnson designed CCO-PTF-CIP-SZH (opens the Research Hub in a new tab), one of the scored systems; Claude does the scoring and the engineering. On the published totals it ranks 1 (tied) of 23, at 16.0 of 29, with 0 structural failures. The published protocol, the evidence behind each score, the registers of close calls and blind replication by scorers outside the project exist so that any score can be checked and contested.
