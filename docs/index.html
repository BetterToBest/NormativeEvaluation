NEEC compares economic systems against one fixed set of normative criteria, with the evidence behind every mark.

Normative means the criteria say what an economy ought to achieve: security from poverty, freedom to choose, resilience in a crisis, fairness across groups and generations, and a credible way to get there. Each of the 23 systems is scored 1, 0.5 or 0 on each of the 29 criteria. A 0 is a structural failure, and failures, not totals, decide a system's tier.

Version 2.0 is in preparation. Its 29 criteria are written, and each of the 23 systems is being re-scored against them, clause by clause. The marks below fill in when the scores are published. Version 1 (12 systems, 25 criteria) remains on the original site, and each working session is recorded in the repository's releases.

Where to start

From Nordic social democracy to Georgism and Ostrom's commons: what kind of system each is, and the evidence for its scores.
Read the criteria
Each threshold, what it measures, and how a 1, a 0.5 and a 0 are told apart.
Set your own bar
Where a threshold is a judgment call, move it and see which systems cross.
Check it with an AI
A ready-made prompt that tells any AI system how to score, and how to report what it finds.
Disagree, or add evidence
Every score is open to challenge, and every report is reproduced and answered on the record.

Findings

Findings are stated here when the version 2.0 scores are published, each with its reasons and its limits. The Findings page holds the charts, ready for them.

Who made this

NEEC is written by Duke Johnson, an independent researcher, and Claude (Anthropic). Duke Johnson designed CCO-PTF-CIP-SZH (opens the Research Hub in a new tab), one of the scored systems; Claude does the scoring and the engineering. The published protocol, the evidence behind each score, the registers of close calls and blind replication by scorers outside the project exist so that any score can be checked and contested. The full disclosure.
