C1.3 Housing Security

Material Security. Can the system provide material necessities reliably and universally?

Change in version 2.0: bar, measure or clause restated (codes K6, R2).

Pass threshold

≥88% housing stability over 5-year periods, housing costs ≤30% of disposable income at 80% of regional median household disposable income

Clause by clause:

  1. ≥88% housing stability over 5-year periods
  2. housing costs ≤30% of disposable income at 80% of regional median household disposable income

What it asks

≥88% housing stability resistant to market volatility, with housing affordable to households at 80% of the regional median disposable income.

Why it matters

Housing represents the largest expense (30-50% of income) and primary wealth-building mechanism. Systems failing to address housing commodification cannot claim comprehensive security. Stability means maintaining housing over 5+ years without involuntary displacement.

How it is measured

Housing stability rate = percentage of participants maintaining stable housing over 5-year periods without involuntary displacement from foreclosure, eviction, or inability to afford rent increases. Affordability: housing costs (rent or mortgage payments plus utilities) no more than 30% of disposable income for a household at 80% of the regional median household disposable income, from the OECD Affordable Housing Database, EU-SILC or national household surveys; for a design, projected on the same measure.

Indicators

household panel surveys for displacement; OECD Affordable Housing Database; EU-SILC; national household surveys.

Benchmark

Community Land Trusts achieve 10x lower foreclosure rates than conventional mortgages (0.46% vs 3.26% during 2008-2010 crisis).

How scores are anchored

1.0, pass

Structural decommodification or strong regulatory stabilization with a documented or clearly-designed stability rate at or above threshold. Centrally Planned Socialism (1.0): "Universal housing provision through state allocation... homelessness essentially eliminated."

0.5, partial

Partial protection or a real but incomplete mechanism — e.g., some cooperative/social housing alongside a still-dominant market allocation. Universal Basic Income (0.5): "Stable income improves housing security... but without housing policy, landlords can capture UBI through rent increases."

0.0, structural failure

Pure market allocation with no stabilizing mechanism. Libertarian Minarchism (0.0): "Pure market allocation with no stabilization... homelessness treated as individual failing rather than systemic outcome."
