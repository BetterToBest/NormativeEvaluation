Method

NEEC asks every system the same question in the same words: does the evidence show that it meets each of {{n_criteria}} stated conditions? This page gives the rules in plain terms. The scoring protocol states them in full.

What "normative" means here

The criteria describe what an economy ought to achieve. Each is derived from stated normative principles, which its page names as its roots. They are claims about what matters, not descriptions of what is typical, so you can reject a criterion or a threshold and still check whether a score follows from it. The Thresholds page lists the judgment calls and their alternatives.

Five domains

Each domain asks one question and holds five or six criteria, each worth one point: material security, human autonomy, system resilience, ethical integrity and implementation viability. Their questions and criteria are on the Criteria page.

Three marks

1.0, pass
The system robustly and structurally meets the criterion's Pass Threshold, every clause shown by named evidence, under normal conditions and, where the criterion specifies it, under stress. A pass says the threshold is cleared, not that the system is perfect.
0.5, partial
A genuine mechanism that does not clear the threshold robustly: it falls short of the figure, holds only under favourable or untested conditions, covers only part of the population, or rests on sources that are genuinely ambiguous.
0.0, structural failure
No structural mechanism addresses the criterion; or the system treats its concern as illegitimate; or the best evidence shows performance far below the threshold, with no credible pathway to close the gap under the system's own logic.

A scorer reasons about the evidence first, then rounds once, at the level of the criterion.

Clause by clause

Most thresholds have several clauses. A 1.0 needs every clause shown met on its own evidence, and a clause the evidence does not address counts as not shown. The rule followed an audit which found that many earlier scores of 1.0 did not show every clause; version 2.0 re-estimates them (decision D28).

Tiers count failures, not points

A system's tier depends only on its number of structural failures: 0 to 2, Potentially Adequate; 3 to 5, Partially Adequate; 6 or more, Structurally Inadequate. A 0.5 is never a failure. So a system with a higher total can sit in a lower tier than one with fewer points and fewer failures, and weighting the domains differently can reorder the totals but never the tiers.

Three kinds of system

Every entry declares its kind before it is scored, because the kind decides how a threshold written for a whole population is read.

  • A mechanism is an institution, instrument or policy family operating inside a wider economy, such as a land value tax or mutual credit.
  • A configured national economy is an existing or historical national economy, scored as configured at a stated date, such as Singapore's state capitalism.
  • A comprehensive system is a design for a whole economy, scored as its own sources specify it.

Evidence

A system in operation is scored from documented outcomes; a historical one from its record, collapse included; a design from its stated mechanisms, with confidence calibrated by the record of the real components it relies on. Sources are spread across the founding literature, peer-reviewed work, implementation records and critics from more than one direction. Where a reasonable scorer could land elsewhere, the scoring document flags the call and names the alternative.

Weights

The totals weight every criterion equally. Appendix A.4 of the Paper tests three alternative weightings; the Thresholds page lets you try them, or set your own.

How the framework grows

NEEC is a living framework. New systems can be scored, and new criteria added as evidence accumulates and new threats to economic security appear. Changes follow the same discipline as scores. A criterion joins only through a recorded decision and a new version of the criteria: version 2.0 added three, on civil liberties and the rule of law, productive and innovative capacity, and harm internalization. A new system is scored in its own document under the protocol, then inserted by a separate maintainers' pass. Every methodological decision is recorded with its reason, in the criteria record and the protocol's decision register; every change is checked by the harness before it is merged; and every working session is published as a release.

Limits

A score is a judgment against a stated threshold, made from the evidence available when the system was scored; it is not a measurement, and evidence dates. A few clauses ask for quantities no agency publishes; for those, a 1.0 needs evidence the scorer names, so in practice they separate only 0.0 from 0.5 until such evidence exists. A comparison across different kinds of system is only as fair as the scope rules that make it, and the protocol states those rules so that they can be contested.
