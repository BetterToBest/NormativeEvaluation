# C4.1: sources the Session 72 blind readings cite but do not cache

Session 72 (ledger S55), the blind re-read of C4.1 on v2.0's two clauses (`blind_s72.py`). The cached files in this
directory are verified by digest (`evidence_cache_s65.py` for the IMF and World Bank files, `evidence_cache_s72.py`
for the rest). The sources below are named, not cached; each reading that rests on one says so.

## Configured economies

- **Singapore.** Government Securities (Debt Market and Investment) Act 1992 (as amended 2017): Singapore Government
  Securities are issued to develop the domestic debt market and the proceeds are invested, not spent; Ministry of
  Finance, "Singapore's Public Sector Debt" (mof.gov.sg): the government's assets exceed its liabilities. Cited only to
  explain the gross figure; v2.0's measure is gross debt and governs.
- **Qatar.** No statutory fiscal rule; Qatar Investment Authority's assets (about US$500 billion, SWF Institute
  estimate, 2025) are not netted against the IMF's gross figure.
- **Timor-Leste** (for Sovereign Wealth Fund Statism's exemplars): withdrawals from the Petroleum Fund exceeded the
  Estimated Sustainable Income in 2008 to 2012 and every year from 2014 (East Asia Forum, "Timor-Leste's financial
  cliff draws closer in 2025", 27 January 2025); IMF, Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste, Debt Sustainability Analysis
  (2017, Country Report 17/360): existing expenditure plans are unsustainable, the Petroleum Fund being depleted in the
  long term given large excess withdrawals.
- **Norway** (Sovereign Wealth Fund Statism): the fiscal rule (handlingsregelen, 2001, revised 2017) caps the
  structural non-oil deficit at the fund's expected real return, now 3%.

## Designs and mechanisms

- **Market Socialism.** Whyte, W. F. and Whyte, K. K. (1991), *Making Mondragón*, 2nd ed., Cornell ILR Press: net
  surplus allocated to the collective reserve fund (at least 20%), the social and education fund (10%) and members'
  individual capital accounts, which stay invested in the cooperative until the member leaves or retires; Italian
  Civil Code, art. 2545-quater: at least 30% of a cooperative's net annual profits to the legal reserve, indivisible.
- **Degrowth Economics.** Daly, H. E. (1991), *Steady-State Economics*, 2nd ed., Island Press, p. 17: an economy with
  constant stocks of people and artifacts, maintained by a low rate of throughput; Kerschner, C. (2010), "Economic
  de-growth vs. steady-state economy", *Journal of Cleaner Production* 18: 544-551 (degrowth in the North as the path
  to a globally equitable steady state); Kallis, G. (2011), "In defence of degrowth", *Ecological Economics* 70:
  873-880.
- **Modern Monetary Theory + Job Guarantee.** Kelton, S. (2020), *The Deficit Myth*, PublicAffairs: the third of the
  six myths, "deficits will burden the next generation" (introduction and chapter 3, "National Debt (Without the
  Baggage)").
- **Universal Basic Income.** Van Parijs, P. and Vanderborght, Y. (2017), *Basic Income*, Harvard University Press,
  chapter 6 (funding by income taxation, the variants not fixed by the mechanism).
- **Universal Basic Services.** Institute for Global Prosperity (2017), *Social Prosperity for the Future: A Proposal
  for Universal Basic Services*, UCL: the services costed at about 2.3% of GDP and funded from taxation; Coote, A.
  and Percy, A. (2020), *The Case for Universal Basic Services*, Polity.
- **Doughnut Economics.** Raworth, K. (2017), *Doughnut Economics*, Random House Business, chapters 6 and 7
  (distributive and regenerative design; agnostic about growth); no public-debt path or saving rule is stated.
- **Fully Automated Luxury Communism.** Bastani, A. (2019), *Fully Automated Luxury Communism*, Verso, chapters 10 to
  12 (public investment in energy and automation; no debt path or saving rule is stated).
- **Libertarian Minarchism, Participatory Economics, Integral, CCO-PTF-CIP-SZH:** cached (`lp_platform_extract.txt`,
  `hahnel_wright_2016_c41_extract.txt`, `integral_wp_v01_c41_extract.txt`, `research_hub_8e8a6ba_c41_extract.txt`).
- **Georgism, Mutual Credit / LETS, Islamic Finance, Ostrom-style commons, Sovereign Wealth Fund Statism:** their
  scoring documents' overviews (the mechanism and its scope), read for what each governs; neither clause's quantity
  is stated there for the mechanism generalised.
