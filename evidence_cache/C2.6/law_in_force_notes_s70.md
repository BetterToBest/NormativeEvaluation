# C2.6 clauses 2-3: law in force, four configured economies (cached Session 70)

Clauses: 2, informed consent to medical treatment protected in law; 3, compulsory treatment/vaccination/quarantine/
isolation prescribed by law, time-limited and open to independent judicial review. Cached by search and, where a
primary text was reachable, by direct fetch; a session with a web browser should verify each citation's current text
before reading, as these are leads, not a completed reading.

## United States (SQ)

Cached primary sources: `us_cfr_42_70.14.html` and `us_cfr_42_71.37.html` (Cornell Law's Legal Information Institute
mirror of 42 CFR 70.14 and 71.37, fetched 2026-10-18). Both require a federal quarantine, isolation or conditional-
release order to be served within 72 hours of apprehension, reassessed no later than 72 hours after service, and
explain the right to a medical review (including an advocate, and appointed representation if indigent); any required
medical examination needs prior informed consent from a licensed health worker; both sections preserve a detained
person's constitutional and statutory right to judicial review. This is a federal administrative rule, not a statute,
and covers only CDC-ordered quarantine/isolation (interstate and foreign); it does not address a general right to
refuse treatment or a vaccination mandate, which are matters of state law and vary by state (found no single federal
statute; Texas SB 1313/HB 2760, a pending 2021 bill, and Illinois and Arizona statutes were found by search but not
fetched -- see search summary, 2026-10-18, queried "United States federal law informed consent medical treatment
statute...").

## China (CN)

Civil Code of the People's Republic of China (2021), Book III (Contracts), Chapter 6 ("Medical Treatment Contract
Liability"), Articles 1218-1228, state informed-consent requirements and consent by close relatives where a patient
cannot consent (Article 1219's "impossible or inappropriate" test, replacing the 2010 Tort Law's narrower "not
appropriate" test); found by search (academic commentary, zjujournals.com, 2026-10-18), full statutory text not
fetched this session. On compulsion: a 2025 study in *Frontiers in Public Health* ("A study of Chinese law on
restricting personal liberty for public health protection: taking the COVID-19 epidemic as the entry point") argues the
Infectious Diseases Law's authorization is broad and its procedural limits are weak, naming no specified decision
procedure or citizen's avenue to contest a lockdown; this is a secondary source's reading of the statute, not the
statute itself, and should be checked against the Law on the Prevention and Treatment of Infectious Diseases'
own text in session 77.

## Singapore (SG)

The Infectious Diseases Act (Cap. 137) governs compulsory quarantine and treatment; a 2021-22 Singapore High Court
judicial review (*Han Hui Hui v Attorney-General* and related cases on COVID-19 vaccination-differentiated measures)
confirms judicial review is available against the government's public-health measures, found by search (vlex.com
case summary, 2026-10-18); the Act's own time limits and consent provisions were not fetched this session.
