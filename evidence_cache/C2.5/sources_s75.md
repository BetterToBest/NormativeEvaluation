Not cached, cited (the designs' and mechanisms' own literature, and the configured economies' law):

- Status Quo (the United States): no exit permit; the expatriation tax (26 U.S.C. 877A) reaches only "covered
  expatriates" (net worth of USD 2 million or more, or high average income tax); Shapiro v. Thompson (1969) and Saenz
  v. Roe (1999) bar durational residency conditions on welfare; SNAP's able-bodied adult time limit (7 U.S.C. 2015(o))
  and the Medicaid community-engagement requirement enacted in July 2025 (Public Law 119-21) condition benefits on
  work; Medicaid eligibility for adults differs between expansion and non-expansion states, so a move between them can
  end coverage.
- Nordic Social Democracy: EEA social security coordination (Regulation (EC) 883/2004) and the Nordic Convention on
  Social Security (2012); activation conditions on social assistance and unemployment benefit in each of the four
  (Denmark's 2024 work obligation for recipients of cash assistance among them); exit taxes on unrealised share gains
  (Norway, Denmark) with deferral for moves within the EEA.
- Centrally Planned Socialism: exit visas; RSFSR Criminal Code article 64 (flight abroad as treason); the propiska
  residence-permit system; article 209 (parasitism).
- China: exit-entry controls (the 2013 Exit and Entry Administration Law, article 12; the collection of passports in
  Xinjiang from 2016, Human Rights Watch reporting); hukou.
- Singapore: the Enlistment Act's exit-permit requirement for national-service-liable males and the bar on renouncing
  citizenship before national service is completed; the CPF's withdrawal of balances on renouncing citizenship and
  leaving permanently; the HDB's five-year Minimum Occupation Period; ComCare's work conditions.
- Qatar: Law No. 13 of 2018 and Laws No. 18 and 19 of 2020 (kafala reforms); the residence permit's tie to an employer
  sponsor; Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International reporting (2023 to 2025) on absconding charges and recruitment
  debt.
- Market Socialism: Roemer, A Future for Socialism (1994): coupons non-transferable for cash, reverting to the
  treasury; Schweickart, After Capitalism (2002/2011): worker self-managed firms with no capital stake, social control
  of investment allocated to regions per capita, a small capitalist sector bounded in size.
- Participatory Economics: Albert, Parecon: Life After Capitalism (2003) and Hahnel, Of the People, By the People
  (2012): no private ownership of productive property or markets; remuneration by effort and sacrifice; membership of
  workers' and consumers' councils.
- Degrowth: Kallis et al., The Case for Degrowth (2020); Hickel, Less Is More (2020): caps on resource use and income,
  working-time reduction, universal public services; none states exit terms.
- Fully Automated Luxury Communism: Bastani (2019); Stakeholder Capitalism: Schwab, Stakeholder Capitalism (2021);
  Doughnut Economics: Raworth (2017): none states exit terms.
- Georgism: George, Progress and Poverty (1879), Book VIII: the land value tax replacing other taxes; the citizen's
  dividend as paired in the design scored.
- Sovereign Wealth Fund Statism (the Alaska model): Alaska Statutes 43.23.005 (eligibility: a full calendar year's
  residency and intent to remain; exclusions for incarceration on a felony conviction).
- Universal Basic Income: Van Parijs and Vanderborght, Basic Income (2017): paid to every member individually, without
  means test or work requirement; the chapter on its political economy discusses limiting it for newcomers (the
  "generosity-hospitality dilemma") as an option, not as part of the definition.
- Universal Basic Services: Institute for Global Prosperity, Social Prosperity for the Future (2017); Coote and Percy,
  The Case for Universal Basic Services (2020): housing, food, transport, information alongside health and education;
  allocation of housing by need, administered locally.
- MMT and the Job Guarantee: Tcherneva, The Case for a Job Guarantee (2020): voluntary, open to all, not replacing
  unemployment insurance or other income support; Mitchell and Wray's buffer-stock proposals, some of which would
  replace unemployment benefit for those able to work.
- Mutual Credit / LETS: Linton's LETS design (1983); North, Local Money (2010): balances in a local unit not
  convertible to national currency; members leaving settle their account.
- Ostrom-style commons governance: Ostrom, Governing the Commons (1990): design principle 1 (clearly defined
  boundaries) and the Törbel commons (rights held by village citizens).
- Islamic finance: AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards on musharaka (No. 12) and mudaraba (No. 13): either partner may end the
  partnership by notice; diminishing musharaka home finance settled by purchase of the remaining units.
