# C2.1: sources the Session 75 blind readings cite (prose; not digest-checked)

Cached by `evidence_cache_s75.py`: `whr_freedom_s75.json`, the Gallup World Poll's "freedom to make life choices"
share by country and year (2005 to 2025) as the World Happiness Report's data application serves it (field FR,
"Freedom (%)": the share answering "satisfied" to "Are you satisfied or dissatisfied with your freedom to choose what
you do with your life?"; the 2026 statistical appendix, cached by Session 70, defines it). Session 70's lead (the
United States at 71%, 2025 edition) matches the application's 2024 value (71.7%); the 2025 value is 75.4%.

Not cached, cited:

- Centrally Planned Socialism (the Soviet-type economy, the corpus's configured case): no Gallup series exists (the
  poll begins in 2005). The readings cite the system's own law: the RSFSR decree of 4 May 1961 "On strengthening the
  struggle against persons avoiding socially useful work" and article 209 of the RSFSR Criminal Code (1975 wording),
  which made systematic avoidance of work ("parasitism") a criminal offence; and the internal passport and residence
  permit (propiska) system (1932; collective farmers held no internal passport until the 1974 statute took effect).
- Qatar: the application's latest Qatar value is 2012; no later Gallup reading is published for it.
- Clause 2 (revealed preference) for the configured economies: no study or record was found that shows acceptance of
  initially refused employment or living situations when an unconditional alternative exists, for any of the six.
  Finland's 2017-2018 basic income experiment (Kela; Kangas et al., 2019) tested an unconditional payment on 2,000
  unemployed people; it bears on Universal Basic Income, not on Nordic Social Democracy's configuration.
- China's household registration (hukou) system: State Council, Opinions on Further Promoting the Reform of the
  Household Registration System (2014); the 2019 guidance lifting restrictions in cities under three million.
- Qatar's sponsorship (kafala) reforms: Law No. 13 of 2018 (exit permits, most workers) and Law No. 18 and 19 of 2020
  (changing employers without a no-objection certificate; minimum wage); ILO Project Office for the State of Qatar,
  progress reports 2020 to 2024, on their implementation.
