# C2.3: sources the Session 75 blind readings cite (prose; not digest-checked)

Cached by `evidence_cache_s75.py`: `oecd_time_use_s75.json` (the OECD Time Use Database, its current SDMX dataflow
DF_TIME_USE, which Session 70 did not locate: it covers the United States, China and the four Nordic economies, not
Singapore or Qatar; each country's latest survey, minutes a day); `eurostat_meaning_of_life_s75.json` (EU-SILC's
"meaning of life" rating, the module's item "Overall, to what extent do you feel that the things you do in your life
are worthwhile?", 0-10, asked in 2013 only; the four Nordic economies); `eurostat_artistic_practice_s75.json` (EU-SILC
2015 and 2022, persons practising artistic activities by frequency: playing an instrument, composing, singing,
dancing, acting, photography, film, drawing, painting, sculpting, handicrafts, writing).

Not cached, cited:

- The United States: the National Endowment for the Arts' Survey of Public Participation in the Arts (2022) reports
  the share of adults who created or performed art in the past year, not weekly frequency; the American Time Use
  Survey (bls.gov, refused this container as it did Session 70's) is the source of the OECD database's United States
  row. No "worthwhile" item on the EU-SILC or ONS wording is fielded for the United States.
- China: the OECD row is the National Bureau of Statistics' 2008 time-use survey (the database's source note); the
  2018 survey is not in the database. No weekly-frequency creative-engagement or "worthwhile" series is published.
- Singapore and Qatar: no national time-use survey in the OECD database; no comparable "worthwhile" or weekly-frequency
  series found.
- Centrally Planned Socialism: the readings cite the system's own doctrine of cultural production -- the 1934 Statute
  of the Union of Soviet Writers (socialist realism as the obligatory method) and Glavlit's prior censorship (1922 to
  1991) -- and the network of state "houses of culture" and amateur collectives through which participation was
  organised.
