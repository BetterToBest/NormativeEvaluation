## Configured economies: the instruments in the configuration at the stated date

- **United States.** No national carbon price or cap. The Inflation Reduction Act (2022) subsidised clean energy;
  Public Law 119-21 (4 July 2025) phases out its clean-electricity and vehicle credits; the executive order of 20
  January 2025 withdrew the United States from the Paris Agreement (effective January 2026). Sub-national caps:
  California's and Washington's cap-and-trade programmes and the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative (eleven states,
  power sector), which together cover a minority of national emissions.
- **Nordic economies.** Carbon taxes since 1990 (Finland), 1991 (Norway, Sweden) and 1992 (Denmark); climate acts with
  absolute targets: Sweden's Climate Act (2017, net zero by 2045), Denmark's Climate Act (2020, 70% below 1990 by
  2030), Finland's Climate Act (2022, carbon neutrality by 2035), Norway's Climate Change Act (55% below 1990 by
  2030). The targets are territorial; the consumption-based series is the clause's measure.
- **China.** The national emissions trading scheme (2021) allocates allowances on intensity benchmarks, not an
  absolute cap; the targets are to peak CO2 before 2030 and reach neutrality before 2060.
- **Singapore.** Carbon Pricing Act 2018: S$5 a tonne (2019 to 2023), S$25 (2024 and 2025), S$45 (2026 and 2027),
  S$50 to S$80 by 2030 (Budget 2022); net zero by 2050 (Long-Term Low-Emissions Development Strategy, revised 2022).
- **Qatar.** Nationally determined contribution (2021): 25% below business as usual by 2030, a relative target; the
  North Field expansion raises liquefied natural gas capacity from 77 to 142 million tonnes a year by about 2030
  (QatarEnergy).
- **Centrally Planned Socialism (the Soviet record).** Micklin, "The Aral Sea Disaster", *Annual Review of Earth and
  Planetary Sciences* 35 (2007): irrigation withdrawals from the Amu Darya and Syr Darya, planned from 1960, halved
  the sea's area and ended its commercial fishery by the 1980s; most of its native fish species were lost. Feshbach
  and Friendly, *Ecocide in the USSR* (1992). The 1980 Law on the Protection of Atmospheric Air set emission norms
  that the plan's gross-output targets overrode. The National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts carry the USSR to
  1991; the figure was not cached.

## Designs and mechanisms

- **Libertarian Minarchism.** The Libertarian Party platform, sections 2.2 and 2.3 (cached,
  `lp_platform_environment_extract.txt`); Rothbard, "Law, Property Rights, and Air Pollution", *Cato Journal* 2(1)
  (1982): pollution as trespass, remedied by injunction or damages where the polluter and the harm can be identified.
- **Stakeholder Capitalism.** Schwab and Vanham (2021); the WEF/IBC Stakeholder Capitalism Metrics (2020), which ask
  for disclosure of emissions and of targets aligned with the Paris goals; the Science Based Targets initiative
  validates absolute or intensity targets for firms that volunteer.
- **Market Socialism.** The Report's design (worker cooperatives in competitive markets, Mondragon); Schweickart,
  *After Capitalism* (2002, 2011): democratic firms maximise income per worker rather than total profit, so lack
  capitalism's expansionary imperative, and the social control of investment can direct it; Ward (1958) and Vanek
  (1970) on the labour-managed firm's restraint. No cap on throughput is part of the design.
- **Universal Basic Services.** Coote and Percy (2020), chapter on sustainability; Hot or Cool Institute, *Universal
  Basic Services and the Fair Consumption Space* (2023): collective provision as a route to lower consumption
  footprints, argued rather than projected on the clause's measures.
- **Ostrom-style commons governance.** Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990); Cox, Arnold and Villamayor-Tomás,
  *Ecology and Society* 15(4): 38 (2010); Ostrom, "A Polycentric Approach for Coping with Climate Change" (World
  Bank Policy Research Working Paper 5095, 2009), an argument for nested action, not a commons institution governing
  emissions.
- **Sovereign Wealth Fund Statism.** The scoring document's overview (the scored mechanism: a non-recurring revenue,
  classically resource royalties, held as a permanent fund); Norway's Government Pension Fund Global, product-based
  coal exclusion (2016) and the climate action plan of Norges Bank Investment Management (2025), which govern the
  portfolio, not national emissions.
- **Georgism, Islamic finance, Mutual Credit/LETS, Universal Basic Income.** Their scoring documents' overviews and
  the Report's UBI overview; George, *Progress and Poverty* (1879), on land as all natural opportunities.
