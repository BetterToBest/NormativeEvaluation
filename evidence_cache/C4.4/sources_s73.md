## Configured economies

- **United States, clause 1's most generous count.** Nonprofit employment: the Bureau of Labor Statistics' research
  data on the nonprofit sector put it at about a tenth of private-sector employment (12.8 million jobs in 2022).
  Cooperatives: about 2 million jobs (Deller, Hoyt, Hueth and Sundaram-Stukel, *Research on the Economic Impact of
  Cooperatives*, University of Wisconsin Center for Cooperatives, 2009). State-owned enterprises outside government
  payrolls: negligible (the Postal Service is counted in federal government payrolls). Nonprofits whose boards
  co-opt their own members are counted in, though the definition would count only member-elected ones, so that the
  count is the most generous one.
- **China.** The Chinese Communist Party's leadership is "the defining feature of socialism with Chinese
  characteristics" (Constitution, article 1, as amended 2018); state-owned enterprises' leaders are appointed through
  the Party's cadre system (the nomenklatura of the Central Organization Department and its provincial counterparts).
- **Singapore.** General election of 3 May 2025: the People's Action Party won 65.6% of the vote and 87 of 97 seats;
  the Workers' Party held its 10. The PAP has governed since 1959. Temasek Holdings is owned by the Minister for
  Finance (Incorporated); the elected President holds a custodial veto over past reserves.
- **Qatar.** The Emir is hereditary (Permanent Constitution, 2004, article 8). The Shura Council's first elections,
  for 30 of 45 seats, were held in October 2021; the constitutional amendments approved by referendum on 5 November
  2024 restored a fully appointed council.
- **Centrally Planned Socialism (the Soviet record).** Collective-farm chairmen were formally elected by the kolkhoz
  general meeting but nominated by the district Party committee and on its nomenklatura (Hough and Fainsod, *How the
  Soviet Union Is Governed*, 1979; Nove, *An Economic History of the USSR*, 1992). Enterprise directors were
  appointed by their ministries; the 1987 Law on the State Enterprise's election of directors by labour collectives
  was dropped by the 1990 Law on Enterprises. Private and cooperative employment outside the collective farms
  remained marginal until the 1988 Law on Cooperation.

## Designs and mechanisms

- **Libertarian Minarchism.** Nozick, *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* (1974): the minimal state of protection and
  enforcement; the Libertarian Party platform (lp.org) keeps an elected constitutional government confined to the
  protection of rights, and places production in privately owned enterprises.
- **Stakeholder Capitalism.** Schwab and Vanham, *Stakeholder Capitalism* (2021): boards' duties to stakeholders,
  with metrics reporting (the WEF/IBC Stakeholder Capitalism Metrics, 2020); no worker election of a board majority.
  The strongest statutory form, Germany's Codetermination Act (1976), gives workers half the supervisory board in
  firms of over 2,000 employees, with the shareholder-side chair's casting vote, so the board answers to owners in
  the definition's sense.
- **Fully Automated Luxury Communism.** Bastani, *Fully Automated Luxury Communism* (2019), its closing part:
  "luxury populism" pursued through elections; municipal and cooperative ownership and public ownership of energy
  and data, with no stated share of employment in accountable organizations.
- **Doughnut Economics.** Raworth, *Doughnut Economics* (2017), chapter 5 ("Design to Distribute"), a menu of
  ownership designs (cooperatives, commons, employee ownership) rather than an adopted one; the scoring document's
  scope decision (`NEEC_DoughnutEconomics_scoring_scratch.md`, overview).
- **MMT + Job Guarantee.** Tcherneva, *The Case for a Job Guarantee* (2020): jobs designed and run by local
  governments, nonprofits and community bodies, federally funded; the programme employs the buffer of those who want
  work and lack it.
- **Universal Basic Income.** Van Parijs and Vanderborght, *Basic Income* (2017): an unconditional individual cash
  payment; the power it confers is the power to refuse (exit), not a place in decisions.
- **Universal Basic Services.** Institute for Global Prosperity, *Social Prosperity for the Future* (2017); Coote and
  Percy, *The Case for Universal Basic Services* (2020): services provided by public bodies and by community and
  cooperative providers, with participatory governance proposed but not specified.
- **Georgism, Islamic finance, Mutual Credit/LETS, Ostrom-style commons, Sovereign Wealth Fund Statism.** Their
  scoring documents' overviews (the scored mechanism and its scope decisions).
