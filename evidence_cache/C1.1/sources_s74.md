# C1.1: sources the blind re-read cites but does not cache (Session 74)

Prose, not digest-checked (`evidence_cache_s74.py` caches the rest).

- Raworth, K. (2017), *Doughnut Economics*, chapters 1 and 5 ("Design to Distribute"): the social foundation's
  income dimension as a target, and the distributive designs (land, money creation, enterprise, knowledge, basic
  income) offered as options, not adopted.
- George, H. (1879), *Progress and Poverty*, book VIII; Larson, W. (2015), "New Estimates of Value of Land of the United
  States", BEA working paper: the order of magnitude of US land rent a citizen's dividend could distribute.
- Alaska Permanent Fund Corporation and Alaska Department of Revenue, PFD amounts 2016 to 2025 (about $1,000 to
  $3,300 a person a year); Norges Bank Investment Management, the fiscal rule (3% of the fund's value).
- Institute for Global Prosperity (2017), *Social Prosperity for the Future: A Proposal for Universal Basic Services*;
  Coote, A. and Percy, A. (2020), *The Case for Universal Basic Services*: the value of services in kind to low-income
  households, stated without a headcount projection.
- Schwab, K. (2021), *Stakeholder Capitalism*; the Davos Manifesto 2020; Business Roundtable (2019), Statement on the
  Purpose of a Corporation: a governance reform leaving the host's transfer system unmodified.
- Seyfang, G. and Williams, C. (2000s), studies of LETS in the UK; Ruddick, W. et al. (2015), the Bangla-Pesa: local
  gains to participants, no headcount at a national line.
- Ostrom, E. (1990), *Governing the Commons*: rules for the appropriation of common-pool resources.
- Singapore: no official poverty line and no PIP survey; Donaldson, J. et al. (2013), "Measuring Poverty in Singapore",
  Asian Social Work and Policy Review.
- Qatar: PIP's one survey (2017); the Wage Protection System and the minimum wage (Law No. 17 of 2020).
